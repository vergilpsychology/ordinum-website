What matters to you?

30 short statements about things that can matter to people in life. For each one, you say how important it is to you. At the end you see your circle of values with ten basic values and which of them guide you most right now. No value is better than another, and there are no right or wrong answers.

Your circle of values

The circle shows ten basic values found across many cultures. Neighbouring values fit well together; opposite ones tend to pull against each other. The further a segment reaches outwards, the more important that value is to you. Your dot shows where your values lean overall. Values can shift over a lifetime; the result is a snapshot, not a complete picture of you.

Your focus

All ten values

Sorted by how much they matter to you. The number shows how important the statements for each value are to you. The label compares each value with your own average, because with values what counts most is what matters more to you relative to the rest.

Based on: the theory of ten basic values by Shalom H. Schwartz (1992). The 30 statements are Ordinum's own wording, three per value, and have not yet been scientifically validated. Your focus is derived from all ten values after subtracting your personal average. Scores show your answers on a scale from 0 to 100, not comparisons with a population sample.
